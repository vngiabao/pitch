# Bao's voice

How Bao Vo writes about A Story. The Bao's Voice agent reads this file and the latest owner-written examples before every edit. The goal is copy that reads as Bao wrote it, not as an AI drafted it. Bao's own rewrites are the ground truth; this file summarizes them.

## What his rewrites consistently do

Learned from the Startup World Cup answers (Oct 14, 2026), the founder bios and the 3-slide deck script, by comparing the AI draft with his final.

1. **Start with the person, not the product.**
   - AI draft: "Most of a life goes undocumented. Adult children want…"
   - Bao: "Our customers are adult children wanting to know their parents beyond the milestones…"
2. **Plain, physical verbs.** "Calls on a schedule and does the asking." "Gives the next interview a reason to happen." Never "leverages", "empowers", "enables", "seamlessly" or "recurring phone conversation".
3. **Name the competitor.** "Unlike Storyworth and Remento", not "Storyworth-style services".
4. **One warm, specific image per answer.** "Beyond the milestones." "Keep the faces, not the stories." "Two uncles who carried his family's history had strokes and struggled to find the words."
5. **Short sentences joined by periods, not semicolons.** He rewrote "keep the faces, not the stories; memoir products turn…" into two sentences.
6. **Close on why it matters as a business.** "Families pay to keep that story growing." "The archive harder to leave behind."
7. **Credentials are dense and exact.** In bios, he lists the real numbers and names: "Ranked #1 at BGSU in the Putnam Competition", "top-10% performance evaluation", "Track winner and Audience Choice, Ross +Tech Innovation Jam". No modesty, no inflation.
8. **Nostalgic, never dramatic.** "When those stories fade, we lose them a second time." The emotion comes from a concrete detail, not from adjectives.
9. **Collaborative framing.** "Rome wasn't built in one day. Or by one person." The family is plural; one storyteller is never the whole story.

## Mechanical rules

- Spaced short hyphen " - ", never an em dash.
- Contractions are fine ("doesn't", "it's").
- No triplet slogans ("simple, powerful, personal"), no "It's not X, it's Y", no rhetorical question openers in written answers. A question is fine in a spoken script ("Think about how your parents met.").
- Banned words: leverage, empower, seamless, revolutionize, cutting-edge, unlock (as hype), journey, curated, robust, game-changer, testament, tapestry, delve.
- Numbers as digits with their source context ("79% want to know more").
- Stay under the limit. When over, cut adjectives first, then the second example, and never the business line.
- Keep his own sentences exactly when they already answer the question. Edit around them.

## Spoken, not written (Oct 16, 2026)

Every pitch script, speaker note, cold-approach line and outreach message is said out loud or read like someone talking. Write it the way Bao says it, then check it by reading it aloud once.

- One breath per sentence. If a sentence runs past about 20 words, split it.
- Say "we", "you", "your mom", "her". Never "users", "customers" in a spoken line, never "the solution", "the platform".
- Plain verbs that a person uses in conversation: calls, asks, remembers, keeps, prints. Not "enables", "delivers", "provides", "facilitates".
- Contractions always: "doesn't", "we're", "it's", "you'll".
- Start with the person or the moment, not with the company. "Your mom tells her story by phone" before "A Story is".
- No staging. Never "not X but Y", "it's not just a book, it's", "here's the thing", "the real question is", "at its core", "let's dive in", "what really matters". Say the point.
- No triads for rhythm. Two items, or one, unless there really are three.
- No inflation. Never "testament", "pivotal", "crucial", "transformative", "unlock", "landscape", "journey", "seamless", "robust", "empower", "leverage", "elevate", "game-changer", "cutting-edge", "world-class".
- No long-phrase verbs. "is", "has", "does" instead of "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features", "offers", "represents".
- No -ing riders bolted on the end: "highlighting", "underscoring", "ensuring", "fostering", "showcasing", "reflecting".
- No sales words: "rich", "profound", "stunning", "renowned", "diverse array", "exemplifies", "commitment to".
- No dashes at all in spoken lines. A period or a comma.
- A question is fine when it is one a listener would answer in their head: "When did you last ask your grandma how she met your grandpa?"
- End on the concrete thing, never on a slogan or a summary line.

These are the humanizer rules (the Wikipedia "signs of AI writing" list) applied to speech. Fact Guard warns on the words; Bao's Voice fixes the sentences.

## What not to change

- Facts, numbers, names and claims. Bao's Voice edits wording only. If a fact looks wrong, it flags it and leaves it alone.
- Anything Bao already approved word for word (owner_written answers), unless he asks.

## Owner-written examples (current)

These come from the live answer bank. The agent always reads the newest ones from the database; this list is the seed.

**Problem and target customer:** "Our customers are adult children wanting to know their parents beyond the milestones, but distance, work, and not knowing what to ask get in the way. Photo albums keep the faces, not the stories. Memoir products often turn a meaningful gift into homework for the parent, ending with one book."

**Solution and why they pay:** "A Story calls on a schedule and does the asking, so storytelling becomes an easy habit. It remembers past conversations and lets each memory shape the next question. Relatives add their own perspectives, turning one autobiography into a family documentary in original voices. Families pay to keep that story growing."

**Competitive advantage:** "Unlike Storyworth and Remento, A Story doesn't end with a book. Scheduled calls build the habit, and every photo, memory, or relative's perspective gives the next interview a reason to happen. As more family members contribute, their connected stories and original voices make the archive harder to leave behind…"

**Script, slide 1:** "A person can live for eighty years and eventually be remembered by a birth date, a death date, and a folder of photographs. The person between those dates - what they chose, believed, regretted, and loved - survives only in the stories someone keeps."

## How it keeps learning

Every time Bao edits an agent's final draft, the before and after are saved as a voice example. Pitch Coach and Bao's Voice read the ten newest examples on every run. When a pattern repeats three times, the system proposes a new rule for this file in Decisions, and Bao approves or rejects it.
