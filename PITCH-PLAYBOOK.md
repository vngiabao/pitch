The standard every A Story pitch, deck and tailored version must meet. The quality bar is the Michigan Business Challenge 3-slide deck (https://claude.ai/artifact/PgnB9KSionF1AVm5EdPF4c), built over many rounds of Bao's direct feedback and revised on October 23, 2026 after judge feedback (section 3). Pitch Tailor and Pitch Coach read this file before every run. Where this file and an older deck disagree, this file wins. Where this file and approved company facts disagree, the facts win.

## 1. What the pitch must make a judge understand

In order, and nothing else first:

1. **The legacy gap.** A whole life ends up as two dates and a folder of photos. What a person chose, believed, regretted and loved survives only in the stories someone keeps. Every retelling changes the story.
2. **Families care - they just keep missing the moment.** 79% of U.S. adults want to know more about their parents' lives before parenthood. Only 37% know their mother's. 74% regret not learning more about relatives who have died. The question comes at midnight, and Mom is three time zones away.
3. **Today's options keep pieces, not the story.** Photo albums: the faces stay, the stories fade. Memoir services like Storyworth: a one-time project. Book done, story over.
4. **A Story is an AI biographer that calls you and your loved ones and tells your stories in your own voice.** It calls, it listens, it saves, it recalls - and each memory shapes the next call. That loop is the habit.
5. **Rome wasn't built in one day. Or by one person.** Relatives add photos, corrections and their own version of the same day. Each version stays side by side and credited. One autobiography grows into a documentary of a life.
6. **It never assumes the story is finished. Today is part of it too.** This is the mission: the everyday, not only the past.
7. **It works, families want it, and we know the next proof.** The product is live end to end. 50 leads, 10 beta families, 5 waiting to join as paying customers. Next: prove that families come back.
8. **How it makes money.** Subscription first, because it keeps the story growing. Books second, whenever the family wants something physical.

If a judge remembers one sentence, it is: *"An AI biographer that calls your parents, and the whole family adds their part."*

## 3. Content rules

- **Judges read each slide in about 35-45 seconds** (MBC judge feedback, October 23, 2026: the product slide was "very hard to follow"). Cut words, make type bigger, and never put text on a slide for everything said aloud. Show a process as a horizontal flow chart with one picture per step.
- **Bao's script is the source of truth for wording.** Adapt length; do not replace his sentences with new slogans. Never import a narrative device from a reference deck (for example a borrowed persona such as "Meet Jamie") unless he asks for it.
- **One idea per slide, and every element serves it.** If a block isn't required by the competition or the argument, cut it ("did they ask for the next proof part?").
- **Concrete over abstract.** A person, an action, a number or a real screen beats a category label. Avoid "living family documentary" when it hides the actual difference.
- **Revenue order:** subscription (keeps the story growing), then books (a chapter, not the finish line), then the tier prices.
- **No prices or "free" on the solution slide.** Prices belong on the business slide only.
- **Team:** two founders. Daniel Hoang Nguyen (CEO) leads product and the product roadmap. Bao Vo (COO and CTO, since October 18, 2026) leads technology, fundraising and strategy, and runs all partnerships, web design, client-facing work and traction. Nhi Tran (CMO) appears only in Michigan Business Challenge materials.
- **Traction is owner-reported early interest:** 50 leads from the first MVP, 10 hand-picked beta families, 5 waiting to join as paying customers for the redesigned release. No arrows between those numbers, no conversion rates, never call the five "paying customers".
- **First sales (October 17, 2026, owner-reported, not yet reconciled):** at a Hope College on-site table, about 10 students reserved A Story for $1 each and one bought a $29 book. Say "about 10" and "first sales"; never round up, never call it a partnership.
- **Product state:** live end to end (calls, family archive, photo uploads, billing), private beta on TestFlight, App Store release in review.
- **Partners and programs:** NVIDIA Inception, Google for Startups, AssemblyAI Startup Program, Deepgram Startup Program and Cartesia Startups Grant are accepted (Google and Deepgram confirmed October 23, 2026). Hope College: students reserved and bought on October 17 (see First sales). They are individual students, never a college partnership.
- **Write " - " (spaced short hyphen), never an em dash.** En dashes are fine in number ranges (7–10 minutes).
- **No placeholders, internal notes, "illustrative" labels or photo disclaimers on slides.** Unknowns go in speaker notes.
- **Urgency without fear.** Calm, warm and plain. No hype words, no "revolutionary", no "it's not X, it's Y" drama.

## 5. Design system: the MBC standard

Canvas 1920×1080. These values come from the 3-slide deck; do not substitute.

**Color**
| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Cream | #F3EBDE | Slide background |
| Card ivory | #FBF7EF / #FAF5EC | Content cards, soft shadow, no border |
| Brown | #3B291D | Headlines, body on light |
| Muted brown | #6E5847 | Secondary body |
| Gold text | #B8741E | The key phrase of a headline, numbering, "Miss" labels |
| Gold line | #C9973A | Dashed timelines, dots, arrows, highlight borders |
| Gold bright | #E0A03F / #EBC86A | Accents on teal, pills on teal, the highlighted number |
| Teal | #2E5163 | Panels, pill labels, numbered badges |
| Teal card | #3A6275 | Cards inside a teal panel |
| Teal rule | #4F7385 | Dividers inside teal |
| Teal body | #D6DEE0 | Secondary text on teal |
| Highlight card | #F1E2C6 with a 3px #C9973A border | The one card that matters most |

Rules: brown with yellow, or teal with yellow. Keep orange to a minimum. Never terracotta and teal together. Toned whites and blacks only.

**Type**
- Montserrat for everything on the MBC system: 800 weight for titles, numbers and card titles; 500–700 for body.
- Hero title up to 100px (the "A Story" wordmark). Slide titles 44–54px; quadrant titles 30–38px with `letter-spacing:-0.5px`. Card titles 24–26px/800. Body 19–23px. Nothing under 18px except badge captions.
- Big numbers: 56–104px/800, cream on teal; gold #EBC86A for the one number that matters.
- Headline grammar: the plain part in brown, **the key phrase in gold #B8741E**. Never italic headings.
- Source Serif 4 is allowed only inside product mock-ups (the book cover, quotes styled as print).

**Components**
- **Pill label** (replaces eyebrows): `padding:7px 20px; font:700 22px Montserrat; border-radius:24px`, teal with cream text on light; gold #EBC86A with teal text on teal panels.
- **Card:** ivory, `border-radius:22–26px`, `box-shadow:0 10px 26–30px #3b291d1f`.
- **Teal panel:** #2E5163, radius 24–26, `box-shadow:0 14px 34px #2e516340`.
- **Stat row:** 100×72 teal icon tile with a custom SVG pictogram that means the number (bars, a cracked heart, two distant homes) → big gold number → bold statement → one-line detail. Thin #4F7385 rules between rows.
- **Numbered step:** teal 44px circle or pill with the number → bold title → one-line detail. Steps connect with short gold `arrow-right` shapes only when order matters.
- **Timeline:** a 3px dashed gold line between two brown date pills, with circular photos (5px cream border) on the line and teal labels under them.
- **Decay row:** portraits, a speech bubble each, and 5-dot rows (filled gold vs hollow sand) showing details lost per generation. This is the "lost in the retelling" pattern.
- **Takeaway line:** 28–32px/800 brown with the final phrase in gold, at the bottom of the slide or panel.
- **Logo:** the A-with-heart mark beside "Story" in gold. Partner badges (NVIDIA Inception, Google for Startups, AssemblyAI, Deepgram, Cartesia) as real logos, small, top right, under "Supported by".

**Layout**
- Dense slides use **quadrants** (Bao's MILEAD pattern): every area carries real content, and figures beat decoration.
- The headline sits at the top-left margin, never centered with the body.
- Page number at the bottom right. No footer rules, no "astoryapp.com" footer bar.
- Fill the canvas. Big empty areas read as unfinished ("too much white space").

**Imagery**
- Real app screens from the app PDF (never invented UI), the A Story image bank (family photos), and the book illustration from astoryapp.com.
- Photos as circular portraits or polaroid stacks with cream borders, never as decoration with no job.
- Prefer figures, graphs, plots and tables over images. A meaningless image is worse than none.

### 5c. Every slide must read without a presenter (Bao, Oct 15 2026)

Decks get read alone: in an application portal, a forwarded PDF, an investor's inbox. A slide has to land with no one talking over it.

- **On the slide:** the claim, stated plainly, plus the evidence or the one moment that shows it. A reader should get the point in a few seconds.
- **In the speaker notes:** spoken punchlines, rhetorical turns, set-ups, callbacks and transitions ("Later rarely comes.", "Imagine being one click away…").
- **Test:** cover the notes and read the slide cold. If the point depends on someone saying it, rewrite the slide line.
- **Show, don't explain:** one universally lived moment (a text thread, a photo) beats a chart that explains. Match the effect of Bao's script, not its exact words.

## 7. What Bao has rejected (never do these)

- Looking like "a report for a finance class": tables of text, lecture tone, research dumped instead of shown.
- Generic AI design: three rounded cards with icons in bubbles, purple-blue gradients, SaaS architecture diagrams, network graphics, waveforms as decoration, circular six-step loops, thin connector-line flows.
- Sparse "premium" emptiness, or a single object floating on a slide.
- Meaningless stock or generated images; icons that don't match the content (an hourglass for "distance").
- Awkward arrows, black bars on photos, misaligned bubbles, text touching other elements, squeezed columns.
- Poorly presented data (pie-like blobs, unlabeled bars). Show ratios instead ("2.2× more voice per dollar", "9% → 30%").
- Orange-heavy palettes; teal and terracotta together.
- Photo disclaimers, "illustrative" labels, page-count claims about the book, war-era framing ("we're not selling to Vietnam War veterans").
- Moving the strong why-now slides into the appendix.
- Bottom footer lines; page numbers anywhere except the bottom right.
- A product slide built as a column of feature cards beside step tiles: judges found it very hard to follow. Use the horizontal flow.
- "Keep" lines in Today's options: show only what families lose.

## 8. Tailoring to a competition (Pitch Tailor)

Inputs: the program's official rules (format, minutes, slide count, judging criteria, required sections, eligibility), the master deck, approved facts and this playbook.

1. **Read the rules first.** Minutes, slide limit, required sections, judging rubric, prize, and whether it's equity or non-dilutive. Quote them in the brief.
2. **Map the rubric to the spine.** Each judging criterion gets one slide or block that answers it explicitly, using the judges' own words in a pill label where natural.
3. **Choose slides from the master deck; never write a new visual system.** A program edition is the master deck reordered and trimmed, plus at most two new slides: the program's "why us / why this program" and the ask.
4. **Fit the time.** Spoken pace is 130–150 words per minute. 1 minute ≈ 1 beat; 3 minutes = the 3-slide compression (problem, solution, proof and business); 5 minutes adds urgency and competition; 10+ minutes uses the full master deck.
5. **Program-specific emphasis:**
   - Voice or AI providers (ElevenLabs, Deepgram, AssemblyAI, Cartesia): voice is the interface, not an add-on. Show a real call. "They give the interviewer its voice. A Story gives it the family context."
   - University or student competitions (MBC, Honors Fund): the founding team and campus tie. Nhi only for MBC.
   - AgeTech and aging (AARP, NIA): older adults' dignity, ease (answer the phone, no app), and family connection. Avoid clinical claims.
   - Investor programs (YC, accelerators, angels): market size, why now, the repeat-use insight, the next milestone and the round.
   - Pitch nights (5x5: five slides, five minutes): legacy gap → want-to-know → A Story → collaborative → proof and ask.
6. **The ask is program-specific**, tied to one result ("$X funds the public launch and the retention test"). Never carry another program's terms over.
7. **Output:** a slide plan (which master slides, in what order, and what changes), a timed script in Bao's voice, a materials checklist (deck, video, one-pager, demo, logos), and the open questions only Bao can answer.

## 9. Coaching lens (Pitch Coach)

Pitch Coach reads like a YC partner who also knows the product: kind, direct and specific. It scores, questions and rewrites. It never flatters.

**Scorecard (1–5 each, with the one change that would raise it):**
1. **Clarity.** Can a stranger repeat what A Story does after 10 seconds?
2. **Problem urgency.** Is the pain felt, specific and frequent, or just sad?
3. **Insight.** Is there a non-obvious reason this works (the call comes to them; relatives' input sharpens the next question)?
4. **Why now.** Cheaper voice AI, older adults using AI, an aging country.
5. **Evidence.** Real numbers, honestly labeled. What's proven and what's next.
6. **Business.** Who pays, how much, why they renew, how they're reached.
7. **Defensibility and competition.** An honest answer to "Storyworth already does calls".
8. **Team and founder fit.** Why these two, why now, full-time commitment.
9. **The ask.** Specific, tied to a milestone.
10. **Delivery.** Fits the time, one idea per slide, spoken in plain founder language.

**Hard questions to drill every time:**
- "Storyworth has phone interviews. Why won't they copy the family part?"
- "What happens in year two? Why does a family renew after the book?"
- "Of your 10 beta families, how many came back for a second call?"
- "What does a call cost you, and what happens to margin at full use?" (Modeled: about $0.32–0.45 per call, $24 left per account at 50% use, negative at full use on a managed stack.)
- "How do you get your first 1,000 families? What's your CAC?"
- "Consent and recording laws for AI phone calls?"
- "Daniel starts a master's in 2027. Are you both full time?"
- "Isn't this a gift product with seasonal spikes?"
- "Why is this a venture-scale business and not a nice app?"

**Coach rules:** answer from approved facts only; when the honest answer is "we don't know yet", say what the test is and when it ends. Flag any claim on a slide that isn't in section 4 or the approved facts.
